# E7: The Distribution-Shape "Problem" Does Not Survive Checking the Right Object

## Conclusion first: no fix needed, and no fix was made

E6 flagged that real win% is close to normal (slightly left-skewed) in
all three recent seasons, while the simulator draws true strength from
a right-skewed lognormal -- calling it "the assumption most likely to
matter for tail-dependent studies" if investigated further. Asked to
fix it. **Investigating it first, before changing anything: the
simulator's actual *output* win% distribution already matches real
data well. The mismatch E6 found was between real win% and the
*input* assumption (true strength), not between real win% and what the
simulator actually produces once a season is played out.** Those are
different comparisons, and only the second one is relevant to whether
any study's results are affected.

## Why the mismatch doesn't propagate

Win% is a bounded [0,1] quantity, averaged over ~36 stochastic game
outcomes per team. That averaging and boundedness compresses an input
distribution's skew substantially -- a team can't win more than 100% of
its games no matter how large its underlying rate advantage, and the
extra "very strong" mass in a lognormal's right tail mostly translates
into "wins nearly every game" rather than a proportionally more extreme
win percentage. This is a real, checkable dampening effect, not an
assumption -- confirmed directly below.

## Checked directly, both strength-assignment methods used in this workspace

**iid strengths** (`assign_true_strengths`, used by E1/S1/S4/original S9), 60 replications on the real 2025-26 schedule:

| | Skew | Excess kurtosis | Shapiro-Wilk rejects normality (p<0.05) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Simulated win% | -0.024 | -0.538 | 5% of replications |
| Real (3-season range) | -0.15 to -0.33 | -0.56 to +0.22 | 0% (none of the 3 real seasons reject normality) |

**Conference-stratified strengths** (`assign_conference_stratified_strengths`, calibrated `conf_log_sigma=0.4`/`team_log_sigma=0.24`, used by the final S9 result `e5c`), 60 replications:

| | Skew | Excess kurtosis | Shapiro-Wilk rejects normality (p<0.05) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Simulated win% | 0.001 | -0.251 | 5% of replications |
| Real (3-season range) | -0.15 to -0.33 | -0.56 to +0.22 | 0% |

Both are close to real data's shape, and both show the same "rejects
normality about as rarely as real data does" pattern. If anything, the
simulated skew is *slightly less negative* than real (closer to exactly
symmetric, where real leans mildly left) -- a small, second-order
difference, not the kind of mismatch that would bias a study's
conclusion.

**Tails, checked separately since bulk-shape statistics can mask
tail behavior** (the object S2's and S4's mechanisms actually depend
on): simulated win% min/max average 0.120/0.878 across 60 replications.
Real: 2023-24 (0.029/0.842), 2024-25 (0.132/0.821), 2025-26
(0.118/0.808). The simulated minimum sits within the 2024-25/2025-26
range (slightly above 2023-24's more extreme outlier season); the
simulated maximum runs a bit hot relative to all three real seasons
(0.878 vs. 0.81-0.84) but not by a first-order amount.

## Why this happened, and the lesson

E6 compared the right two things in isolation (real win% shape vs. the
stated modeling assumption) but stopped one step short of checking
whether that mismatch actually reaches anything a study measures. This
is worth naming directly: it is the same category of error this
workspace has now caught multiple times in the *other* direction (a
calibration that looks fixed on a superficial check but isn't, e.g. the
e5b sigma miscalibration) -- here it very nearly went the other way,
almost prompting a change to fix a problem that a closer look shows
doesn't exist. The general lesson for this workspace, stated plainly:
**always check the downstream, actually-used statistic before treating
an upstream assumption's superficial mismatch as something to fix.**

## What this means for prior findings

Nothing changes. E6's other findings (win% spread overshoot, conference-
effect instability across seasons, OT-rate undershoot) are unaffected
and still stand as real, checked gaps. This one specific item --
distribution shape -- is resolved as a non-issue, not fixed, because
there was nothing in the simulator's actual behavior to fix.
